      Financial Principles

      GENERAL EDUCATION — NOT PERSONALIZED FINANCIAL ADVICE

      These are widely-cited general principles, not a recommendation for your specific situation. For advice tailored to you, talk to a licensed financial advisor.

      • Build an emergency fund covering 3–6 months of essential expenses before investing.
      • Pay down high-interest debt (credit cards, high-rate loans) before most investing — the "return" is the interest rate you stop paying.
      • Diversify across asset types rather than concentrating in one stock, sector, or property.
      • Time in the market generally matters more than timing the market for long-term goals.
      • Review your net worth and spending regularly — small recurring costs compound quietly.

      Assistance & Support

      GENERAL POINTERS — CHECK YOUR OWN GOVERNMENT/COUNCIL SITE FOR ELIGIBILITY

      Programs, amounts, and eligibility change often and vary by country/state — this is a starting list of categories to search for, not a guarantee you qualify.

      • Family/child payments — search "family tax benefit" or "child benefit" for your country.
      • Child care subsidy — many countries subsidize a % of care costs for working/studying parents.
      • Rent/housing assistance — public housing waitlists, rent relief, or bond loan schemes.
      • Utility relief — energy/water providers often have hardship or concession programs — call and ask.
      • Free financial counseling — many countries fund free, non-judgmental debt counseling (e.g. Australia's National Debt Helpline) — a real person who can look at your actual numbers.
      • Local community/charity organizations often help with food, school costs, or emergency bills — a local council or community center is usually the fastest way to find out what's available.
